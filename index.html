How to Use BranchMath

  1. Set up once. Go to Business Settings and enter your company info, your real labor and equipment rates, overhead, and the profit margin you want. The defaults are sensible starting points for a small (2–4 person) tree service — tune them to your actual costs.
  2. Build the bid. Enter the customer and job details, then add one entry per tree. Crew time auto-fills from the tree's size and work type; adjust it to match your crew. Tick the hazard boxes that apply (dead, leaning, near a structure, near power lines) and set access difficulty — each one raises that tree's cost by a fair multiplier instead of you guessing a pad number.
  3. Trust the three numbers. Break-even is what the job costs you, all-in. Never bid below it. Recommended adds your target profit margin — that's your normal bid. Premium (+15%) is for rush work or when you're booked out and the job has to be worth displacing another.
  4. Save every bid. Saved bids live on the Saved Bids tab. Mark them Won or Lost as you hear back — over a season, your win rate at your recommended price tells you whether your margin is right.
  5. Send the quote. The Customer Quote tab turns the current bid into a clean sheet with your company info, per-tree line items, the total, and your terms. Print it or save it as a PDF and hand/send it to the customer.
  6. Send it in one tap. Add the customer's phone and email in the Bid Builder, then use Send on a saved bid (or the Send card on the Customer Quote tab): Email, Text, and WhatsApp open in your own apps with the itemized quote already written; Copy quote text pastes anywhere. Saved bids always send at their frozen snapshot prices.
  7. Invoice with Wave. The Wave card on the Customer Quote tab shows the quote as invoice lines (item, qty, price). Copy them, open Wave, and paste them into a new invoice — an open-and-paste handoff, not a live sync: this file works offline and never connects to your Wave account. Track the result back in the Bid Builder's Payment Tracking card (status, method, deposit).
  8. Your rate card. Business Settings holds your preset services & items with your own prices (plus any custom items you add). New bids pick them up from the Preset Services & Items card; override a price on a single bid without changing the preset. Changing a preset never reprices a bid you already saved.
  9. A photo for each tree. On each tree entry, tap Add a photo to take or pick a photo of that tree — add as many as you need, remove or replace one with a tap. Photos are shrunk on your device, saved with the bid, and printed on the customer quote beside that tree's line so you (and the customer) know exactly which tree is which. Filling in Where on the property, stump and wood choices, the landing / drop zone, and a one-line note (under More details) makes the quote clearer still.
  10. Two figures, kept separate. Each tree shows This tree by itself — just that tree's labor and equipment at your rates with its difficulty multipliers, no disposal, travel, extras, or overhead. The Whole job, everything in figures in the Pricing Breakdown are the full job the way BranchMath has always figured it: every tree plus disposal, travel, extras, overhead, and your margin (break-even / recommended / premium).
  11. Close out every finished job. Open the saved bid, scroll to Job Closeout — Bid vs. Actual, and enter the actual crew time, your actual other costs (disposal, equipment, fuel, rentals), and the amount you collected. BranchMath figures your actual margin and shows how the real hours and costs landed against the bid — and the Saved Bids summary rolls every closed-out job into one real-margin figure, so you can see what your bids actually finished at.

How the math works

Per tree: crew time × crew size × labor rate, plus crew time × equipment rate — then multiplied by that tree's hazard and access multipliers. Add disposal, travel (miles × your per-mile rate), and extras for the direct cost. Overhead % goes on top of direct cost to get your break-even. The recommended bid divides break-even by (1 − margin), which is true margin math (not a markup), so a 25% margin really is 25% profit in the price. Premium is recommended × 1.15. If sales tax is on, it's added on top and shown separately. Closeout math: actual labor is actual crew hours × crew size × your labor rate; add your actual other costs for the actual direct cost, apply the same overhead % for the fully loaded actual cost, and the actual margin is (amount collected − actual cost) ÷ amount collected.
